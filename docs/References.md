# Mathematical context and references

The principal statement has the hypotheses and algebraic Jordan conclusion of
the spectral isometry conjecture in Mathieu–Schick, Conjecture 4.6, and
Mathieu, Problem 5. A spectral isometry here is a complex-linear map preserving
the spectral radius of every element. The main statement assumes surjectivity
and preservation of the identity and concludes bijectivity and preservation
of every square. It applies to arbitrary complex unital C*-algebras.

An algebraic Jordan isomorphism need not preserve adjoints: inner similarities
provide examples. This distinguishes the conclusion from the Jordan
*-isomorphisms in the norm-isometry setting of Kadison's theorem.
